The battle has 2 mode, scenario mode (usually has to be completed in epic tab); the generic mode could be done any time in the battle tab.

The battle supported Player vs Player up to 5 people and Player vs Computer also up to 5 people.

# Game rule

## 1. Game Setup.

Game could be configured All era or Single era. The Single era game only uses location cards and great people cards from the chosen era. The All era game draws locations from every era and Great People from the whole of history, starting at the era it opens on.

Every game starts at a real year (see §9, Real history). A Single era game opens on its era: the year its earliest-born Great Person was born. An All era game opens on a start era (random unless chosen) and moves forward through history from there.

Whatever era history opens in, the first screen asks each player to pick one Great Person from 3 candidates (fewer, equal for everyone, when a small era cannot offer a full table 3 each); the computer takes its strongest. The candidates are the earliest-born of the pool from the opening year on, shuffled between the players, so the table starts as contemporaries. Each player also gets 1 Event card and 1 Follower card (contest prizes bring more). A player holds one Great Person at a time, their Great Person slot; slot legacies (First Empire, Kingmaker, New World Patronage, Mother of Russia) add slots, and each new slot is filled with a pick from people born later, founding a lineage of its own (§9). All cards start as hand cards.

The board opens on 4 Location cards (cities) of the age history opens in, revealed and set on the table. The cities always belong to the current age: when history moves into a new age (an age opens with the birth of its first Great Person), every city becomes one of the new age, and the cards and running states on it stay. City legacies (Circumnavigation, Seven Seas) add a city for every player while in effect; set aside, the board shrinks back as soon as a city is empty.

The board will look like a table, with column displayed as different locations. rows displayed as different players.

## 2. Game Objective.

Each Great People card has a Legacy (§10). When a Great Person completes the achievement that unlocks it, the player claims the legacy. There is no points race: the game runs to the end of history, and the player with the most legacies wins.

## 3. Turn based action.

Each Player could do several actions in a turn.

### A. A Player could bring a Great People Card on board, allocate it to a location.
### B. A Player could add a Follower to a location that already has Great People on.
### C. A Player could move a Great People Card / Follower Card from a location to another.
### D. A Player could start an event on the location he/she is on. Some event is global but most are local only happens on the location.
### E. A Player could retrieve a Great People Card / Follower card back to hand.
### F. A Player could claim the legacy of one of the Great People on board who completed his/her achievement, thus get the legacy (§10). Each legacy can be claimed once per player.
### G. A Player could attack the other Player if both some of their cards are within the same location, this only happened in local events.
### H. A Player could end a local event that happening on this city 
### I. A Player could update the public or private status of a card
### J. A Player could retire one of his/her Great People (in hand or on board) at any time on his/her turn (§9).
### K. A Player could switch one earned legacy into or out of a legacy slot (§10).

Each Player could do only 1 time A, 1 time B and 1 time K each turn, but can do any number of C, D, E, F, I or J (though each Great People and Follower only 1 time). Only a Great Person in his/her career (§9) can be brought on board. 
Also if a Great People or Follower just get on board, he/she cannot do C/D/E/F/I.
Each Player could do G any times.
A round is each player play 1 turn.

## 4. Card private or public.

When a card is onboard, he/she could be in private or public status. In public it is face on, in private it is face down. 
When face down, other player cannot know what this card is.
After an attack happened, both player's card in this location must be revealed as public.
When a card is bring to the board, he/she could decide if it initialized as public or private.

## 5. Scenarios.

Each stat has 3 different scenarios, the 1st is local conflict, the 2nd is local survival, the 3rd is global competition

### politics: coup, Terrorist Attack, international conference
### strength: battlefield, riot, olympics
### culture: art critique, censorship, roadshow
### wealth: businese war, Depression, auction
### technique: Tech Race, Sanctions, World Fair
### intelligence: Argument, puzzle, Expedition
### belief: Miracle, Inquisition, pilgrimage
### reputation: Lawsuit, Scandal, Celebration

Special: natural hazard (has several different types: flood, blizzard, storm, earthquake, fire, tsunami, pandemic, drought)

Local Event: Only work in this location, if a card is stronger in this dimension than the other, they could attack the other. The winner(some times the defender) will decide if they would like to kill the loser: 
If killed, the card will be sent to discard pile; if not, the losing card will be forced to be retrived to the Player owns them.  
If there is a draw, then nothing happens.
Note: in a challenge, we should compute the total points of all the Great People and Followers in the same location. 
For example if player A has 2 Great People cards and 2 Followers in a art critique scenario, the total should be GP1's culture + GP2's culture + any follower bonus.

Local Survival: Any player in this location with corresponding point (all GP and followers together) less than 10 will be killed and moved to discard pile.

Special Survival: Any player in this location with total point (all GP and followers together) less than 100 will be killed and moved to discard pile.

Special Survival is random happened, not bring onboard by player. It always lasts 1 round.

City states: besides natural hazards, a city can fall into any local scenario by itself. At the start of every year, each location with no scenario has a 30% chance to enter a random one: a local event (lasts 3 years), a local survival (2 years) or a natural hazard (1 year), each type equally likely. Scenarios started by players keep running until they are ended or replaced. Global competitions are only ever started by players.
In real card game, the city state is drawn from the supply deck for every empty location; in programmable game the computer rolls it.

Global Competition: All players involved in this. These scenario always last for 1 round.
By the end of this round, whoever player has the highest total value of the stat (for the specific stat, culture e.g.) over the board (that are public)
The strongest of the Global Competition will get the bonus of event cards (2), follower cards (4), or GP card (1) to his/her hand.

The Scenario card will keep working until it is replaced by another event or some GP end it. 

## 6. Getting new Great People card

New Great People only arrive as successors (§9). When a Great Person dies, the player chooses a successor born later than the current year. A slot legacy founds a new lineage the same way. Contests pay in cards, not people: winning a fight in a Local Event (attacking or defending) or a Local Survival (surviving while a rival there is swept away) earns 2 Followers or 1 Event card, and the Global Competition doubles it to 4 Followers or 2 Event cards. Each new year has a 5% chance that a global competition breaks out by itself.
Claiming a legacy no longer brings a new Great People card (the Epic fight lessons, which are not on the timeline, keep the old rule of drawing one).

## 7. Turn Order

Great Person Onboard: Deploy a Great Person to the field.

Follower Onboard: Deploy Followers to the field.

Manual Retrieve: Any card may be retrieved by player from board.

Relocation: Any card may move to a different location.

Change Private/Public Status: Any card may change from private to public or vise versa.

Attack: Execute attacks using Great Person cards. To conduct attack, the GP card has to be public, besides any public Followers and GP belong to the same Player will also automatically join the attack. The defense side will automatically have all its card at the same location revealed as public.

Survival Resolution: Calculate the outcomes of Survival scenarios.

Event Resolution: Calculate the outcomes of Event scenarios.

Manual Scenario Termination: A Great Person can end a scenario if they possess the matching Identity (e.g., an Artist can terminate "Cultural Criticism").

Initiate New Scenario: Players could start a new scenario if he/she has the scenario card.

Achievement Check: Determine if a Great Person has completed an achievement; claiming it earns the legacy.

New Year: after every player has played, the year advances by 1: Great People who outlived their death year die, empty cities may fall into a city state, and income legacies pay out.

## 8. When to End a game

The game runs to the end of history: the year 2030, or sooner when no player has a Great Person now or to come (in hand, on board, retired, or a successor still to choose). Then the player with the most legacies wins; a tie goes to seat order.

There is no early win by points. A player whose lineages have all ended keeps his/her legacies and waits: while the other players still have Great People, play goes on, and if no human player has a Great Person left, the computer plays history out quickly (§9, time skip) before the legacies are compared.

(The Epic fight lessons, which are not on the timeline, still end when a player reaches 5 winning points.)

## 9. Real history

One round is one year, in every era.

Every Great Person card carries a birth year, a death year (none for someone still living, who lives until the timeline ends in 2030) and a career start age (18 unless stated; prodigies start earlier, e.g. Michael Jackson at 6, Clara Schumann at 9, Johannes Brahms at 10, Pakal the Great and Gian Lorenzo Bernini at 12, Miyamoto Musashi at 13, Marie Antoinette at 14, Diego Maradona at 15, Hua Mulan at 17).

- A Great Person enters the game in the year he/she was born. Before the career start the card stays in hand and cannot be brought on board.
- A Great Person in his/her career can be played normally.
- When a Great Person outlives his/her death year, he/she dies of old age, wherever he/she is (hand, board or retirement). A Great Person killed in play (by an attack with Kill on, a local survival or a natural hazard) dies too.
- When a Great Person dies, his/her lineage continues: the player chooses a successor from the Great People nobody has claimed yet who are born later than the current year. The 3 nearest by birth are offered (more with successor legacies). The computer chooses the strongest of its options at once. If no one born later remains, the lineage ends.
- Retire: a player may retire any of his/her Great People at any time on his/her turn. A retired Great Person leaves play for good but does not die: the successor is only chosen when the retired Great Person reaches his/her real death year.
- Time skip: whenever no human player has a Great Person in his/her career (everyone is dead, retired, not yet born or too young) and no human still has a successor to choose, time jumps ahead year by year until a human Great Person's career begins. During the skipped years Great People age, die and are succeeded, running scenarios wear off, and the computer's Great People on board play out their lives quickly: each has a 5% chance per year to win a fight towards its legacy, and claims it when unlocked. The skipped years are summarised when play resumes.

## 10. Legacy

Each Great People card has a Legacy in place of its old Achievement. A legacy has:

- a name (the achievement's name, e.g. Gandhi's Satyagraha);
- how it is earned: the achievement text on the card, and the rule the game checks: win a number of fights (1 for every card at present) in the legacy's stat — attack and kill an enemy Great Person in a local event of that stat, whose own value in that stat is at least 5;
- an effect, designed from the figure's history (e.g. Satyagraha: +3 to your total whenever you defend; Imhotep's Pyramid: your Great People live 15 years longer; Amelia Earhart's First Solo Atlantic: your cards ignore natural hazards).

Effects are one of these kinds:

| Kind | Effect |
|---|---|
| contest | +N to your total in contests of a stat (or any stat), when attacking, defending, in survival checks (local survivals and hazards), in global competitions, or in every contest |
| follower | each of your Followers (of a stat, or any) that counts in a contest adds +N more |
| hazard shield | your cards ignore natural hazards |
| longevity | your Great People live N years longer |
| prodigy | your Great People start their careers N years earlier (never before age 4) |
| successors | +N choices whenever you pick a successor |
| income | every N years you gain an Event card or a Follower |

A claimed legacy counts towards victory (§8) and belongs to the player for the rest of the game, even after the Great Person who earned it dies. A player has 2 legacy slots: only the legacies in slots are in effect. A newly earned legacy fills a free slot; once both are full, the player can switch one legacy in or out per turn, so not every legacy can be in effect at once. Each legacy can be claimed once per player.
